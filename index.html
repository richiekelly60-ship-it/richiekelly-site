Why clients bring me in

A timing
problem.

"Most problems blamed on functional safety are really timing and clarity problems."

By the time functional safety is introduced on most programmes, design decisions, assumptions, and timelines are already fixed. At that point, the task is correcting the project rather than guiding it.

That is where rework appears, costs rise, and pressure builds on the team. My work is focused on avoiding that situation — defining what is needed early, keeping it connected as the project develops, and reaching design approval and handover with fewer surprises.

The cost of getting this wrong

The pattern
repeats.

On most projects where functional safety arrives late, the same sequence unfolds: requirements are retrofitted, traceability is reconstructed, and the team spends the final phase correcting documentation rather than confirming design. The direct cost is rework. The indirect cost is the pressure, the timeline slip, and the credibility of the people accountable for delivery.

The earlier functional safety input arrives, the less of that pattern you have to manage. Whether you are at concept stage or approaching a major design review, the right input now is worth more than none — and more than more resource.

Why experience at this level matters

Richard
Kelly.

Over more than 20 years working in nuclear environments, I have supported complex programmes across design assurance, safety assessment, and safety management. That experience has made one pattern very clear: projects rarely fail because people lack capability. They fail when requirements are unclear, accountability is diffused, and assurance activity is bolted on instead of built in.

I hold dual certification as a Certified Functional Safety Expert (CFSE, Exida) and TÜV Functional Safety Engineer — and I have spent over 20 years applying IEC 61508 and IEC 61511 on live projects, not reviewing them from a distance. That matters because the difference between a consultant who knows the standards and one who has delivered against them under real project pressure is exactly the difference that shows up at design approval.

I have worked with both major contractors and specialist engineering teams on nuclear programmes involving ONR regulatory oversight, including work against NS-TAST-GD-003 and NS-TAST-GD-046. Today I use that experience to give engineering leaders the clarity and confidence to move through design approval and handover without the last-minute scramble.
